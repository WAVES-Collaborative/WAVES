# WAVES Authorship Guidelines

This document provides all contributors to WAVES research activities
with transparent definitions and procedures regarding standards for
authorship for WAVES related publications. While the authorship
guidelines are written with reference to the context of works submitted
to peer-reviewed journals, the guidelines are intended to cover all
works, including conference abstracts and proceedings and Knowledge
Mobilization outputs.

## General terms of engagement

- In working together, we will operate a standard ‘no surprises’ rule of
  consortia. Furthermore, groups that are contributing to an ongoing
  WAVES work agree to not directly compete with the collaborative
  efforts and thus we ask that any potential conflicts be declared prior
  to participating.

- Open communication will be established through regular calls and email
  updates for all collaborating investigators.

- Collaborators will not use the information/data provided on calls in
  advance of publication without prior discussion and agreement of the
  group.

- Wider contributions to the analysis group and proposed subprojects are
  actively encouraged and proposals can be made and discussed at calls.

## WAVES Authorship Guidelines

In general WAVES follows the ICJME guidelines for authorship. An author
must make:

1.  Substantial contribution to the concept or design of the work; or
    the acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of data for the work;
    AND
2.  Drafting the work or revising it critically for important
    intellectual content; AND
3.  Final approval of the version to be published; AND
4.  Agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work, ensuring
    that questions related to accuracy or integrity of any part of the
    work are appropriately investigated and resolved.

Specifically:

1.  The WAVES Team has developed and maintains a Manuscript Matrix of
    publications. This document indicates a lead author who will oversee
    developing each work and meeting the publication timeline.
2.  For the 3 core manuscripts, the lead and senior author positions
    have been decided and listed in the matrix. (i.e., step counts,
    MVPA, sedentary time).
3.  Any member of the WAVES Team (including, and especially trainees)
    can propose additional publications to the rest of the team. If
    agreed, these proposed publications will be added to the Manuscript
    Matrix and become the responsibility of the lead author.
4.  An overarching goal is to ensure first and last authors for each
    manuscript will have primary affiliations from different
    institutions.
5.  Individuals who make a substantial contribution to a WAVES study
    should be given the opportunity to be part of the authorship
    process. All authors should be identified before a work is started.
    Note:
    1.  Ghost or relinquished authorship (i.e., when a person makes a
        substantial contribution to the research or manuscript but is
        not listed as an author) is not permitted.
    2.  Gift or honorary authorship (i.e., the addition of an author who
        has not made a substantial contribution to the research or
        manuscript) is not permitted.
    3.  Planted authorship (i.e., where an individual has been named as
        author without his or her knowledge or consent) is not
        permitted.
6.  We will aim to offer up to 3 authorship positions for each
    validation dataset that is contributed to the work.
7.  Contribution of a validation dataset means you agree meet the
    requirements study intake form.
8.  For projects with more equal contributions across investigators, up
    to 2–3 additional collaborators may be included under “…and the
    WAVES Collaborators.” These additional collaborators will have the
    opportunity to review the manuscript if they request to do so and
    will be listed as collaborators (e.g., indexed on PubMed). Project
    lead (primary author and senior author) and the majority of authors
    should agree on the publication venue (e.g., the targeted journal)
    as early in the process as possible.
9.  Author order should be determined as follows, unless the venue of
    the publication mandates otherwise:
    1.  First author should be the individual who has made the most
        substantial contribution and/or taken the lead in writing the
        manuscript.
    2.  Additional authors should be listed immediately after the first
        author in the order of the declining magnitude of the
        contribution; when several authors are deemed to have made a
        comparable contribution, names will be listed in alphabetical
        order.
    3.  The individual who has overall responsibility for the reported
        study should be listed last.
10. The lead author’s responsibilities include:
    1.  Inviting all individuals who have made a substantial
        contribution to a WAVES study to participate in the authoring
        process.
    2.  Leading the author team in the writing process.
    3.  Integrating the various components of the manuscript so that the
        parts collectively present a coherent work.
    4.  Preparing a short, written description of each author’s
        contributions to the manuscript, and distributing the record to
        all authors to read, approve, sign, and return to the lead
        author for safekeeping.
    5.  Communicating with authors during the writing and editing
        process.
    6.  Confirming authors prior to the manuscript’s submission.
11. A secondary lead author should be identified in the case that the
    lead author decides to step down from their leadership role or is
    unable to meet the manuscript’s deadline as stated in the WAVES
    Manuscript Matrix.
12. If a lead author does not meet the timeline, the second author
    should have the opportunity to take the lead.
13. The corresponding author should be identified as early as possible.
    The corresponding author commits to:
    1.  Obtaining written permission from all acknowledged individuals
        to be acknowledged in the work.
    2.  Collecting and confirming the correct and desired spelling of
        author names
    3.  Collecting accurate contact information and other information
        (e.g., Open Researcher and Contributor Identification) as
        requested by the submission protocols.
    4.  Being available throughout the submission and peer review
        process.
    5.  Responding to the journal’s editorial team in a timely manner.
    6.  Regularly communicating with co-authors regarding review
        progress.
    7.  Being available after publication to address critiques and
        requests for further information.
14. Contributors should be contacted for permission to be acknowledged
    in any publication, presentation, or other material before a work is
    submitted to a publication for consideration.
15. All authors should be involved in the review process.
16. All authors should approve the final manuscript before it is
    submitted for publication.
17. Should a dispute regarding authorship arise, authors should first
    attempt to resolve the issue together. If that fails, WAVES co-leads
    (See above) will assist with conflict resolution. As a last resort,
    a third party (someone not involved in the manuscript) will be asked
    to assist.

## WHO IS INVOLVED IN A WAVES PUBLICATION?

The following describes the 3 types of involvement in the WAVES project:

| Title | Description |
|----|----|
| Author | An author is a person who has made a substantial conceptual or material contribution to the contents of the publication or document. An author takes responsibility for at least one component of the work and should be confident in the overall integrity of the document. A lead author or authors are responsible for managing the writing process. A corresponding author is responsible for the submission, review and follow-up process after publication. |
| Guarantor | A journal may request that one or more authors be recognized as a public guarantor of the work. The guarantor is publicly accountable for the accuracy and integrity of the submitted work and research protocols (including data storage), and should be able to speak to the individual contributions of the work’s authors. A guarantor may or may not be listed as an author. |
| Contributor | A contributor is a person (or institution) who does not meet the definition of an author but who has contributed to WAVES research. This person (or institution) will be acknowledged in the publication for their contribution to the study. Examples of contributors include financial supporters, providers of technical and administrative services, project advisory committee members, community partners, and individuals assisting with access to study participants or materials. |
